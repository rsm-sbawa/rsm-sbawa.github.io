Pick the model that makes money, not the one with the best score

Three marketing cases taught me the same thing: accuracy is a means, profit is the point.

Model scores like accuracy and AUC are useful for comparing models, but nobody gets paid in AUC. In my customer analytics course, three cases made that point from different angles.

Intuit: compare models in dollars

Intuit wanted to mail a second wave of QuickBooks upgrade offers. My team compared logistic regression with a small neural network. The neural network had the better AUC (0.747 vs 0.708), but that wasn't why we chose it. Mailing the top 20% of ranked businesses in the test data, it earned $29,655 versus $23,655. That's $6.59 of profit per piece mailed, and about $5.03M projected across the businesses still to be mailed.

Tuango: check the decision against what actually happened

Tuango, a deals app, had to choose which of 397,252 customers should get a push message for a karaoke deal. Every message costs money, so I worked out the breakeven response rate, about 9 to 10%, and only targeted customers whose predicted chance of buying was above it. That came to 174,126 people.

The best part of this case was that Tuango had actually sent the deal to everyone, so I could check my rule against reality instead of trusting my own projections. On the real results, messaging only the customers above breakeven earned 887,000 RMB, against 112,000 RMB for messaging everyone. Return on marketing spend went from 3.1% to 56.6%.

FiTech: what customers like best isn't always what pays

FiTech was testing 12 credit card offers across 3 credit-risk groups. One offer was objectively the best for customers: lowest rate, no fee, fixed rate. It wasn't the most profitable everywhere. We tested all 36 offer and group combinations on 4,000 people each, scored each on expected profit per email (response rate times customer lifetime value, minus the email cost), and rolled out the winner in each group. The rollout made $846,000 against an $835,000 forecast, and $877,000 in total across both rounds.

The common thread

  • Decide what one more correct prediction is worth, and what one more mistake costs, before choosing a model.
  • Use a breakeven rule so every message, mailer or offer has to earn its cost.
  • When you can, test the decision against real outcomes, not just the model's own estimate.
  • Report the business number first. The model score is supporting evidence.
